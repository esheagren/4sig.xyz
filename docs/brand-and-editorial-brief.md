# Four Sigma: brand and editorial brief

Working brief, September 8, 2026. Records the founder’s direction and translates it into principles for future question selection, product copy, design, and other materials. The short promise and public positioning below were adopted for the website on September 8, 2026; editorial procedures remain working recommendations.

## Purpose

Four Sigma brings important quantitative information into focus. It helps people build and maintain a numerical understanding of the world through four carefully chosen questions a day.

The central editorial question is: **What are the most important numbers for understanding the world over the last five years?** A broader north star is the roughly 3,000 numbers a broadly educated person should be familiar with. This is a way to think about the collection’s value and breadth, not a fixed production quota.

The quality of the questions is the principal reason to return. Each should feel worth knowing, even to someone who has no interest in their score. Estimation makes the player engage with a fact before seeing it; choosing a range also develops awareness of uncertainty. Those mechanics serve the editorial purpose.

## Audience

Literate, curious people interested in how the world works, particularly the non-engineering technocratic audience found in places such as Washington, DC, and New York. They may read The Economist or Our World in Data and enjoy New York Times games. Assume curiosity and intelligence without assuming specialist training.

This audience informs the level and selection of the material. It should not require insider vocabulary, professional credentials, or familiarity with a particular social circle.

## What belongs in the collection

Most questions should concern what the founder calls **meta-facts**: important quantities whose values change over time and whose approximate scale an informed person needs to refresh. Population is the clearest example. Other subjects include economics, technology, especially AI, urbanism, China, public health, and accessible biomedical science. These are starting areas, not an exhaustive taxonomy or a set of quotas.

A smaller share should provide durable reference points: constants and other useful yardsticks that help people reason about scale. The distance between Earth and the Sun is an example. Such questions earn their place through explanatory usefulness; entertaining trivia alone is insufficient.

The last five years establish the center of relevance. They do not exclude an older baseline that makes a current number meaningful. Prefer the latest reliable observation to an unsupported claim about “right now.”

Our World in Data and Metaculus are reference points for the spirit of the product: care with quantitative information, evidence, definitions, and uncertainty. The relationship is an editorial inspiration, not an affiliation. Four Sigma can act as a kind of anti-news: attention to consequential conditions and changes that matter beyond the day’s headlines.

## Working editorial test

Before admitting a question, answer these questions:

- **Why should someone know this?** State the aspect of the world that becomes easier to understand. “It is surprising” is not enough.
- **What does the number help explain?** It should illuminate scale, prevalence, change, a constraint, or a consequential comparison.
- **Is the quantity well defined?** Specify the population or geography, unit, denominator where relevant, and reference period. Avoid prompts that imply more certainty or consensus than the evidence supports.
- **Can the answer be supported and maintained?** Use a credible, inspectable source with enough context to interpret the number. Record when the observation applies and when it should be reviewed.
- **Will the reveal repay the player’s attention?** Give the answer, its source and date, and a short explanation of why it matters. A useful comparison can make it memorable.

Editorial selection should be deliberate. It should not select or frame numbers merely to confirm a preferred political story. A surprising result is welcome when the quantity matters and the evidence is sound.

Illustrative subjects, not yet researched questions: urban housing supply, the scale and composition of China’s economy, population aging, AI adoption, energy use, and the burden or prevention of major diseases. Each still needs a defensible measure, scope, date, and source before becoming a playable question.

## Keeping changing facts trustworthy

Treat a recurring quantity as an editorial subject with dated observations, rather than a timeless fact whose answer silently changes. Question records should support a source, measurement period, verification date, definition notes, and a review date appropriate to the source’s release cycle. These are editorial requirements to consider in future data work, not a claim that all fields already exist in the application.

Repeat important subjects when the learning value or updated observation justifies it. Refreshing a player’s understanding is part of the purpose. Do not manufacture variety with obscure facts simply to avoid all repetition.

When a source revises an observation, preserve the question and answer that were used for completed games. Exclude or explicitly frame quantities too contested or imprecise for fair numerical scoring.

## Brand and language

The feeling should be smart, careful, clear, and inviting. Mathematical precision and the warmth of the existing Mathematical Espresso direction belong together. Avoid military imagery, intelligence-test bravado, specialist posturing, or the dryness of a reference manual.

Lead with what people gain: a better sense of the world and its proportions. Explain estimation and confidence as the way the game works. Public-facing copy should not make obscure trivia, guessing prowess, or a score the whole promise.

“Meta-facts” is a useful editorial term; players do not need to learn it to understand the product. Prefer plain language such as “numbers that matter,” “how the world is changing,” and “a sense of scale.” Use these concretely rather than as unsupported claims of importance.

**Positioning:** Four Sigma is a daily game of the numbers that matter for understanding the world.

**Short promise:** A better sense of the world, four numbers at a time.

The menu introduction, About tab, page metadata, and shared-score invitation use this direction. The private design gallery remains a historical record of earlier explorations.

## Implications for future work

- Review the existing bank against importance and explanatory value before treating its size as a measure of progress.
- Develop coverage across consequential subjects and useful comparisons; avoid four arbitrary facts bundled into a daily edition.
- Give answer reveals enough editorial care to teach something worth retaining. Keep the interface concise while making sources and context accessible.
- Judge question quality partly by whether a player would want to remember or discuss the answer after the game.
- Keep the broader collection coherent over time. The desired result is accumulating understanding, not merely accumulating scores.
